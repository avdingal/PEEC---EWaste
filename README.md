## 📖 About the Advocacy

The **PEEC Initiative** is an environmental awareness project developed as an academic requirement for **MO-ENV076 H2101 (People and the Earth's Ecosystem)**. 

Our mission is to confront the rapid acceleration of electronic waste (e-waste) in the Philippines by educating households, students, and local communities on responsible consumption, product longevity, and safe disposal pathways.

---

## 🎯 Milestone 1 Focus: What's the Issue?

For this initial milestone, the platform examines the systemic issues behind e-waste accumulation:

* **Scope & Categorization:** Defining Waste Electrical and Electronic Equipment (WEEE), focusing closely on Category 6 (Small IT & Telecommunications equipment like smartphones, chargers, and tablets).
* **Root Drivers of Waste:**
  * **Short Product Lifespans:** Aggressive hardware replacement cycles and planned software obsolescence.
  * **Barriers to Repair:** Glued parts, non-replaceable batteries, and proprietary components that make fixing devices economically unfeasible.
  * **Surging Consumer Demand:** Accelerated multi-device ownership driven by remote education and digital lifestyle shifts.
  * **Collection Gaps:** A critical deficit in accessible municipal drop-off infrastructure, leaving hazardous electronics in household storage or ordinary trash.
* **Vulnerable Demographics:** Highlighting how students and youth face disproportionate financial and technical strain from rapid digital updates and mandatory device upgrades.

## 🛠️ Milestone 2 Focus: What To Do? (Evaluating Solutions)

Milestone 2 transitions from diagnosing the crisis to systematically evaluating existing interventions across the Philippines based on feasibility, operational scale, sustainability, and limitations:

1. **Refurbishing & Component Repair (Highest Potential Circular Model):**
   * *Status & Operations:* Carried out through formal IT Asset Disposition (ITAD) facilities (e.g., Envirocycle, Casterly) and informal repair stalls. Viable units undergo secure data sanitation, battery/screen replacement, cleaning, and re-grading for resale or donation.
   * *Ecological Impact & Assessment:* Extends device lifespan, prevents heavy-metal leaching, and recovers embedded silicon value. Philippine climate-tech asset refurbishment diverted over 250,000 kg of e-waste (~116,300 laptops) in 2024 alone. However, formal refurbishment remains heavily concentrated in corporate IT assets, leaving consumer electronics mostly reliant on informal shops.

2. **Dedicated Drop-Off Kiosks & Mall Collection Points:**
   * *Status & Operations:* National programs implemented in high-traffic shopping and commercial hubs, including over 120 Globe Telecom collection points and 88 SM Supermalls/Cyberzone drop boxes.
   * *Ecological Impact & Assessment:* Eliminates friction by allowing ordinary citizens to dispose of small gadgets during daily mall errands. However, collection alone does not guarantee recycling; participation is strictly voluntary, and the country operates only 91 accredited treatment, storage, and disposal (TSD) facilities.

3. **National Regulatory Policies & Governance (RA 6969, RA 9003, DENR DAO 2013-22):**
   * *Status & Operations:* Hazardous waste management and transport require DENR-EMB clearances and formal facility registration under DAO 2013-22.
   * *Ecological Impact & Assessment:* Provides legal authority to inspect commercial facilities and impound illegal international shipments (such as the 234 containers intercepted at Subic Port since March 2025). However, policy remains the weakest link: the Philippines still lacks a dedicated, standalone e-waste law to regulate consumer electronics and informal recyclers.

4. **Commercial Trade-In & Buyback Programs:**
   * *Status & Operations:* Retail mechanisms run by brands like Samsung PH and Globe where working gadgets are appraised in-store or online for trade-in discounts, postpaid bill credits, or GCash payouts.
   * *Ecological Impact & Assessment:* Offers a direct financial incentive to retrieve hoarded electronics from household drawers. However, trade-in discounts risk encouraging faster upgrade cycles, and companies provide little to no public data on whether traded devices are actually refurbished or recycled responsibly.

* **Synthesis:** Existing solutions are functional and convenient, but their total effectiveness is bottlenecked by voluntary participation and an undersized domestic recycling infrastructure. Refurbishing remains the most sustainable circular pathway.
